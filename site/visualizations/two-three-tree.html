CHAPTER 18 SUBSTITUTE / 2–3 TREES

When a node fills, a key rises.

A 2–3 tree stores one or two keys in each node, every internal node has two or three children, and all leaves remain at the same depth. Insert keys and watch full nodes split and promote their middle keys upward.

Insertion events

    Why the tree remains balanced

    Insertion searches to a leaf, maintaining ordered key ranges. A two-key leaf can temporarily acquire a third key. Split the three into a left one-key node and right one-key node, and promote the middle key into the parent. If the parent overflows, repeat recursively. A root split creates a new root, increasing the height by one while keeping all leaves at the same depth.

    Search and insert: every internal level has at least two children, so height grows logarithmically with the number of keys. Each insertion performs O(log n) local work, plus splits along at most one root-to-leaf path. This teaching tool implements insertion only; the lecture's deletion/redistribution illustrations are a separate future module.
