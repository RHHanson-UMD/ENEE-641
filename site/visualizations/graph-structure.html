Proof checklist

  • DFS timestamps d[v] and f[v] must respect nested discovery/finish intervals. Low[v] is the smallest reachable discovery time via a tree-path below v followed by at most one back edge.
  • A nonroot v is an articulation point if some DFS child u has low[u] ≥ d[v]. A DFS root is an articulation point if it has at least two DFS children.
  • A tree edge (v,u) is a bridge precisely when low[u] > d[v]. Biconnected edge components are split at appropriate low-link boundaries; the original slide explicitly treats edge-based equivalence classes.
  • A connected undirected graph has an Euler tour exactly when every vertex has even degree. The construction traverses each edge once; disconnected non-isolated components prevent one global tour.
  • For a directed graph, DFS additionally has forward and cross edges. The current calculation focuses on the undirected lecture algorithms; do not apply the bridge criterion unchanged to directed graphs.

Boundary tests: A cycle has no bridges; a tail creates bridges; two triangles meeting at one vertex have an articulation without a bridge; two disconnected cycles have even degrees but no global Euler tour.
