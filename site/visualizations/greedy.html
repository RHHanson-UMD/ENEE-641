CHAPTER 16 / GREEDY ALGORITHMS

Take the locally best choice.

Two independent Chapter 16 examples: finish-time greedy interval scheduling and least-frequency-first Huffman coding. Every decision can be inspected, and the results are compared against exact small-instance computations.

1. Activity selection — earliest finish first

Each bar is a lecture requiring the same room. Meetings can share a room when the next start time is at least the previous finish time.

Exchange intuition: Some optimal schedule can include the earliest-finishing available lecture: replacing its first lecture with the earliest finisher does not reduce room availability for later lectures. Repeat on the remaining feasible intervals.

2. Huffman coding — merge the two least frequent

Change the relative frequencies. Each step combines the two lowest-weight nodes to create a parent node; traversing left/right edges produces prefix-free bit codes.

Frequency queue and merge

    Final symbol codes

    Correctness intuition: The two least frequent symbols can be siblings at maximum depth in an optimal binary prefix tree. Merge them into a pseudo-symbol, solve the reduced instance optimally, and split that pseudo-symbol back into the original pair. The total weighted bit length increases by exactly their combined frequency.

    Why the greedy choice is safe

    Greedy algorithms need a problem-specific exchange argument, not just an attractive local rule. Chapter 16 proves optimality for lecture scheduling and Huffman coding. Neither proof claims that arbitrary optimization problems can be solved greedily.
