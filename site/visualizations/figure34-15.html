Why the equivalence holds for every subset

Take a graph G with n vertices and choose any subset K of k vertices. Its complement graph has an edge precisely where G does not. Therefore K is a k-clique in G if and only if V \ K is a vertex cover of size n−k in the complement of G.

If K is a clique, no complement edge can have both endpoints in K; each complement edge is touched by V \ K. Conversely, if V \ K covers every complement edge, every pair of vertices inside K must be joined in G. This is the entire reduction and works in both directions.

The source example has clique {u,v,x,y} and complement cover {w,z}. The two alternative graphs are original practice cases. A candidate that is not a clique gives a complement candidate that is not a cover: experiment by unchecking and checking vertices.
