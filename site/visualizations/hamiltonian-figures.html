Figure 34.16 — Edge widget

Lecture slide 33. For edge (u,v), the reduction creates six vertices on each of two sides, with vertical edges and four crossing edges. External connections attach at the four end vertices. Try the two 12-vertex traversals and two six-vertex side traversals; a selected cover vertex can traverse its incident gadget side.

Cross edges are drawn as original thin teal lines; highlighted amber edges trace the chosen valid traversal. Changing direction or partial/full traversal supplies comparable original walkthroughs of the exact source gadget.

Figure 34.17 — VERTEX-COVER to HAM-CYCLE

Lecture slide 34. Source graph: vertices w,x,y,z with edges wx,xy,wy,wz; source cover {w,y} and k=2 selectors. Every original edge becomes a 12-vertex widget. Each selected cover vertex traverses its incident widgets. A single selector separates adjacent vertex paths in a Hamiltonian cycle.

The input graph is redrawn independently. Choose vertices below to test a cover or expose an uncovered edge.

Proof connection: A vertex cover of size k yields k selector-linked paths whose concatenation visits every widget vertex exactly once. Conversely, each gadget must be traversed through at least one endpoint's path, so selected vertex paths cover every source edge. This page constructs and checks small forward witnesses; it does not solve arbitrary HAM-CYCLE instances efficiently.

Figure 34.18 — Weighted traveling-salesperson tour

Lecture slide 36. Source complete graph on u,v,w,x has weights uv=4, ux=3, xw=5, vw=2, uw=1, vx=1; the textbook's minimum-cost tour has cost 7. Select any tour and compare its cost to the independently enumerated small-instance optimum.

Exact TSP enumeration here is only for four-city instructional examples. It has factorial growth and is not a polynomial-time solution to the general decision problem.
