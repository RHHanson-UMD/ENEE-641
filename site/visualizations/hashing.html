CHAPTER 11 / HASH TABLES

Hashing, collisions and universal families

Based on the actual lecture source Chapter11Sept22-2026, slides 2–22. This site uses original text and computations, not copyrighted textbook diagrams. Chaining and probing are distinct collision-resolution methods. The load factor is α=n/m.

Separate chaining

Under simple uniform hashing, unsuccessful and average successful chained search take expected Θ(1+α), but the worst case can be Θ(n) for colliding keys. Compare a balanced choice of keys with deliberately colliding ones.

Open addressing — lecture slides 17–18

The source explicitly inserts 89, 18, 49, 58, 9 into a 10-slot table using h'(k)=k mod 10. Compare linear h(k,i)=(h'(k)+i) mod 10 against quadratic h(k,i)=(h'(k)+i²) mod 10; quadratic probing may revisit slots and fail to find a free one.

Universal hashing — lecture slide 12

The source defines hₐ,ᵦ(k)=((ak+b) mod p) mod m, with prime p>m, 1≤a<p, 0≤b<p and key 0≤k<p. The lecture example p=17, m=6, a=3, b=4, k=8 gives h=5.

Additional lecture coverage

  • Division hashing k mod m; avoid relying on lower bits when m is a power of two.
  • Multiplication method takes fractional part of kA before scaling to m.
  • Universal families bound collision probability for distinct keys by 1/m with random independent function choice.
  • Double hashing requires a step relatively prime to m; open-addressing deletion uses DELETED markers rather than a naive empty slot.
  • Perfect hashing uses two levels for static keys and chooses a secondary table size proportional to each bucket count squared.
