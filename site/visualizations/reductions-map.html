Why the direction of an arrow matters

If A ≤ₚ B, then a polynomial-time algorithm for B would also solve A in polynomial time: transform an A instance into B, solve it, and reuse the answer. Conversely, if A is already NP-hard, this reduction helps establish NP-hardness of B. Membership of B in NP must also be proved before concluding that B is NP-complete.

Polynomial-time reductions compose: if A ≤ₚ B and B ≤ₚ C, then A ≤ₚ C. A chain in the graph represents this composition. The highlighted path is one route to a result, not a proof that its individual arrows are valid; those reductions require separate mathematical constructions.

The two additional graphs are deliberately abstract exercises, not statements that named problems are NP-complete. Try selecting a destination that cannot be reached from the start to see why reversing an arrow is generally invalid.
